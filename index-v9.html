Samsung Health India
  • iF Design Award 2026 · Winner
  • Best Project 2025 · Samsung Design Delhi

Samsung Health’s medication tracking was designed globally, around assumptions that didn’t hold in India’s pharmacy and healthcare ecosystem. I adapted it for India around a partnership with Tata 1mg, working out which touchpoints had to change to carry verified partner data, and which new features that data made possible.

Alongside it, I designed the UX for Samsung’s Personal Health Records under India’s Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, with Eka Care: onboarding and record management, inside tight government infrastructure constraints. The India insights went back to the global Samsung Health team, reversing the usual direction.

Samsung Design Delhi · Visual design · Exploration, 2024
Galaxy Watch Faces

Two briefs for the next Galaxy Watch. For analogue faces: find what gives traditional watches their art-deco character, beyond the craftsmanship. For photo faces: turn a static picture with the time on top into something personal, contextual and composed for a round screen.

The research was a team of four. The four faces, Pop Analogue, Pop Demure, Simple Photo and Fish Eye, I designed end to end, and all four are in Samsung’s consideration library for a future release.

Polygon Design Inc. · Brand identity & visual system · 2021
Rebranding Polygons
  • As seen on Shark Tank, 2026

Polygons makes everyday products that fold flat, starting with a measuring spoon that becomes three sizes depending on how you pick it up. The product took off faster than the brand around it, so before it reached the market, the identity needed a second look.

Working in-house with the founder closely involved, our team streamlined the identity rather than replacing it, and built it from real applications (packaging, web, billboards, social) instead of a rulebook, so anyone joining later could pick it up and use it. In 2026, Polygons pitched on Shark Tank with the brand up on the screen behind them.

Interaction design · Shipped in One UI 6, 2023
In-Call Notes

People were leaving active calls to open Samsung Notes, writing down numbers, addresses and action items, then hunting for the way back. The behaviour already existed; the experience just wasn’t designed for it.

The Phone app and Samsung Notes belonged to different teams, so the work was as much alignment as design. I reframed it as a bridge between two existing products: notes open from the call with its controls in view, carry the caller and time automatically, come back right after you hang up, and stay linked to the contact.

Interaction design · Shipped, since 2022
Business Caller ID

Samsung’s in-house answer to spam calls. Samsung partners directly with network carriers to verify that a call really comes from the business it claims to be, and shows the reason for the call, provided by the business itself.

From 2022 I took it beyond its US launch to new carriers, each with its own requirements: some wanted their network name beside the call message, others didn’t. I designed the incoming-call screen and notification touchpoints to meet each carrier’s needs while staying clear and concise for people.

Visual strategy · Research, 2024
One UI 7 Icons Strategy

For years, One UI’s icons have been bright squircles with flat white symbols: simple, consistent and instantly Samsung. But recognisable isn’t the same as intuitive, and across the industry icons were turning back into objects with depth, material and a story.

With a team of three, I benchmarked iOS, stock Android, Nothing, Xiaomi, Huawei, Oppo and Microsoft, app by app, through three lenses: container and grid, shape and metaphor, colour and material. The case we made, for essential but expressive icons that keep One UI’s clarity, set the direction for the One UI 7 icons.

Visual direction · Exploration, 2022
Future One UI Visual Design

Galaxy devices are functionally exceptional, yet to Gen Z they feel practical rather than desirable. Competitive analysis of iOS, Nothing OS and Pixel, and user perception studies, pointed to four differentiators: visual clarity, intentional restraint, a cohesive language and subtle personality.

Instead of refining screens, I audited the whole ecosystem (every app, widget, notification and system interaction) to find where coherence broke down, then designed the feeling first and let the interface follow. The output is a reference framework to guide product work for years.

My part
Ecosystem audit, visual direction
